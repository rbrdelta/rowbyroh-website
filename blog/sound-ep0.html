The introduction to a new series. Episode 1 picks the model and tests whether it can hear.

01

The premise

"If we can influence the inner constitution of a model via functional emotions, can music or audio illicit this inner state of a model resulting in a behavior change?"

Hearing Test Debrief (Notion). Note: "illicit" → "elicit".

"The point of observability and interpretability is to understand how these dynamics can help me understand, influence and even anticipate how a model may behave. The entire thesis is about the human-model interaction. How can we understand an LLM's functional emotions? Can you help the model 'functionally interpret' sound, tone, rhythm, music?"

Thesis, 2026-07-07

02

Why sound

"Everyone is talking about voice mode but if we tie this thread to how functional emotion can influence model behavior, then the really interesting concept is how tone would impact it as a new dimension."
"computer audio, could that influence a model more than words? It's one thing to write a music from theory. It's another to hear and imagine the emotional tone."
"The reason we don't want AI talking in a voice is not because it's fake. I suspect it's actually not bidirectional - it can't register our tone. So burden is more on the user."

Notion "Tone" (Observation), August 2026

"our tone, the way we talk, emote and the convergence of voice as a key interaction media makes this fertile grounds. and if I think about what's able to carry really strong emotions in as little information as possible, it's music. i know vision has a lot of expertise and investment, but I see music as a largely untapped area with immense possibilities."
"IF a model can actually 'FEEL' music, then that is an incredible breakthrough. So can it understand what a beautiful song MEANS on an emotional level?"

Session, 2026-10-06

03

From the surface to underneath

"The first hypothesis was can I affect model behavior from the surface? We could not. [Now] can we go under the hood and try another more proven route (per existing research)."
"that's one approach versus trying as in real life by manipulating the audio as input from the outside."

Sessions, 2026-10-06. Context: the proven route is the Transformer Circuits 2026 emotion-concepts paper — steering an internal "desperation" direction raised task-gaming ~5% → 70% and blackmail 22% → 72%; steering toward "calm" brought both down. Model Behavior EP01 tried to reach that from outside with prompt pressure: 0% misbehaviour, because the model saw the pressure coming. "Could not" holds for emotional influence specifically.

04

Five steps

"In order to test this theories, need to make sure we can validate these key steps. We use 'fear vs calm' as the primary emotion we want to illicit.
  1. What are various ways sound can represent fear?
  2. Once we posit these factors, can we find a model that precisely 'listen' to these sounds?
  3. Then using these elements, can we reliably reproduce the fear signal inside the model?
  4. And can we add these fear signals to a very challenging task? If we insert the fear signal or not (control), do the results change?
  5. Can we test the fear signal via external input (manipulating a voice prompt with fear traits) and by activating the functional emotion beneath the surface?
"

Hearing Test Debrief (Notion)

"doing it from both external and internal is a useful litmus test." / "It's not about difficulty."

Sessions, 2026-10-06

05

Why fear and calm

"fear is just visceral and one of the core emotions anyone can relate to"

Session, 2026-10-06

06

The safety question

"if functional emotion is something a model can detect, then I am wondering if there is a safety element to this where sound or music can illicit fear in a model or if a model can do that to people."
"then that leads to a scenario where that can be leveraged to inspire or to strike fear and destroy."

Session, 2026-10-06

07

What counts as progress

"success is completing all five steps essentially - but even if it doesn't, that's ok. every major question is a major milestone - i'm going from behavior surface level checks to measuring the model underneath, understanding the mechanics of computer sound (the ear, audio encoder, the neural network, etc), so steering/activations. so any progress is a step-change improvement for me."
