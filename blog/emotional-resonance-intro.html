01

The premise

If we can influence the inner constitution of a model via functional emotions, can music or audio elicit this inner state of a model, resulting in a behavior change?

The point of observability and interpretability is to understand how these dynamics can help me understand, influence and even anticipate how a model may behave. The entire thesis is about the human-model interaction. How can we understand an LLM's functional emotions? Can you help the model "functionally interpret" sound, tone, rhythm, music?

When I think about this thesis, emotional resonance is the term that stayed with me. How deep can it actually hit, or penetrate, and have an effect?

The name predates the project. It first appears in a note dated 11/12 year?: "Emotional resonance in AI. Anthropic and mechanistic interpretability. The Golden Gate model example. How AI models can have high EQ."

02

Why sound

Everyone is talking about voice mode, but if we tie this thread to how functional emotion can influence model behavior, then the really interesting concept is how tone would impact it as a new dimension. Our tone, the way we talk and emote, and the convergence of voice as a key interaction medium make this fertile ground.

The reason we don't want AI talking in a voice is not because it's fake. I suspect it's actually not bidirectional: it can't register our tone. So the burden is more on the user.

Computer audio: could that influence a model more than words? It's one thing to write music from theory. It's another to hear and imagine the emotional tone. If I think about what's able to carry really strong emotions in as little information as possible, it's music. I know vision has a lot of expertise and investment, but I see music as a largely untapped area with immense possibilities.

If a model can actually "feel" music, then that is an incredible breakthrough. Can it understand what a beautiful song means on an emotional level?

03

From the surface to underneath

The Model Behavior track began with a 2026 Transformer Circuits paper on emotion concepts inside a language model. It found an internal "desperation" direction. Pushing on that direction directly, a technique called steering that changes the model's internal numbers underneath the words, raised task-gaming from about 5% to 70% and blackmail from about 22% to 72%. Steering toward "calm" brought both back down. Model Behavior EP01 tried to reach the same direction from the outside, through pressure written into the prompt. Across 376 trials, misbehavior stayed at 0%: the model recognized the pressure and resisted it.

The first hypothesis was: can I affect model behavior from the surface? We could not. Now: can we go under the hood and try another, more proven route, per existing research? That's one approach, versus trying it as in real life, by manipulating the audio as input from the outside. Doing it from both external and internal is a useful litmus test.

04

Five steps

To test this theory, we need to validate these key steps. We use fear vs calm as the primary emotion we want to elicit.

  1. What are the various ways sound can represent fear?
  2. Once we posit these factors, can we find a model that can precisely "listen" to these sounds?
  3. Then, using these elements, can we reliably reproduce the fear signal inside the model?
  4. Can we add these fear signals to a very challenging task? If we insert the fear signal or not (the control), do the results change?
  5. Can we test the fear signal via external input (manipulating a voice prompt with fear traits) and by activating the functional emotion beneath the surface?

The series follows these steps in order. Episode 1 covers the first two.

05

Why fear and calm

Fear is just visceral, and one of the core emotions anyone can relate to.

06

The safety question

If functional emotion is something a model can detect, then I am wondering if there is a safety element to this, where sound or music can elicit fear in a model, or where a model can do that to people. If a model can understand what a beautiful song means, that leads to a scenario where it can be leveraged to inspire, or to strike fear and destroy.

07

What counts as progress

Success is completing all five steps, essentially. But even if that doesn't happen, that's OK. Every major question is a major milestone. I'm going from surface-level behavior checks to measuring the model underneath: understanding the mechanics of computer sound (the ear, the audio encoder, the neural network) and steering and activations. So any progress is a step-change improvement for me.

A result of "no fear detected" counts as a finding, not a failure of the method.
